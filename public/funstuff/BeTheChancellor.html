Model checked against HMRC's ready reckoner

Post it

How this works. A synthetic income distribution — lognormal body, Pareto tail (α = 2.04) — fitted to HMRC's projected 2026–27 counts: 40.8m income tax payers, 7.7m at the higher rate, 1.29m at the additional rate. It reproduces total income tax of about £341bn against an OBR forecast in the £340s, and reproduces HMRC's ready-reckoner costings to within roughly 10% on the allowance and the basic-rate limit.

What it leaves out. National Insurance, dividend and savings rates, Scottish and Welsh devolution, benefit interactions, and any effect on GDP. Rate changes at the top are where a model like this is least reliable and where the honest answer is a range, not a number. Treat the output as an order of magnitude, not a costing.

Sources: HMRC, Direct effects of illustrative tax changes (June 2025); HMRC, Income Tax liabilities statistics; OBR, Economic and fiscal outlook.
